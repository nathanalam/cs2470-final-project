From the records of 2026-11-09 to 2026-11-19.

- **OpenJev reaches steady state at concurrency 8 on every big GPU.** Per-GPU utilization goes from 64-70% at concurrency 1 (93% on the A100) to 98-99.7% at 8, and questions/s rises by at most 3.5% beyond that while mean latency doubles with each step. At concurrency 8 it answers **2.22 q/s on the A100 80GB PCIe, 6.77 on the H100, 7.03 on the H200 and 12.60 on the B200**, with accuracy 78.6-78.9% on all four (1,000 questions each).
- **H200 = H100 + 4%, B200 = 1.86x H100.** The H200 has the H100's compute and 1.4x its memory bandwidth, so near-equal throughput at full utilization says OpenJev on full JEVal states is not memory-bandwidth-bound: prefill compute sets the speed.
- **Few batch series reach B\*.** Of the batch models, only Qwen3-8B, Kev-4B and the two 9B models reach kernel_busy >= 0.95 at a batch that has stopped gaining throughput: Qwen3-8B (B\* 4 on H100 and H200, 1 on the A100), Kev-4B on the L4 (B\* 8 at 128 tokens) and the A100 (16), and the 9B models on the L4 (B\* 2-4 at 128 tokens, 1 at 1024). Laya never reaches 95% in any mode on any GPU (best 0.92, on the L4): its throughput levels off while the GPU still idles between kernels.
- **LFM2-8B-A1B is held back by host prep, not the GPU.** With inline prep it levels off at kernel_busy 0.87 (H100, H200) to 0.92-0.93 (B200, A100). Pretokenized on the H100, the same model reaches **0.97 at B\* = 32 (128 tokens, 120 decisions/s) and 0.98 at B\* = 16 (1024 tokens, 49/s)**, 10-17% more throughput than inline's plateau. Forcing the eager expert loop instead of grouped_mm costs more than prep does: kernel_busy 0.66-0.71 at B = 64 and still rising, so its B\* lies past the largest batch run.
- **On the L4 every candidate saturates the GPU at small batches**, so the choice is about accuracy. Kev-4B (fused + graphs) is both the most accurate (70.9%) and the fastest candidate at 128 tokens (15 decisions/s at B\* = 8; LFM2 13, the 9B models 5-6). d1-3B is next (65.3%) but has no batched path. Qwen3.5-9B and JevK5-9B score 56.6% with 49 of 200 invalid (75.0% on the answered rows, approximate); they also run out of memory at B = 64 (128 tokens) and B = 32 (1024 tokens). LFM2 is least accurate (42.9%, ECE 0.315) and just misses B\* (kernel_busy 0.947 at B = 4).

### Read with care

- The two speed units do not mix: batch rows are decisions/s on 128- and 1024-token states, and OpenJev rows are questions/s on full JEVal states (up to 16k tokens).
- The A100s differ: the 2026-11-19 runs are on an **A100 80GB PCIe** (300 W), the 2026-11-08 runs and all batch records on an **A100-SXM4-80GB** (400 W). The 11-08 SXM4 run's 2.49 q/s at concurrency 8 (200 questions) does not compare directly with the PCIe card's 2.22 (1,000 questions).
- **Kev-4B at 1024 tokens on the H100, H200 and B200 sits at kernel_busy 0.22-0.32 and 10-14 decisions/s at every batch size**, no faster than on the A100 (0.82-0.86, 14/s). Batching does not help it there, so something outside the GPU limits it. It needs a trace before any claim about it.
- Qwen3.5-9B and JevK5-9B have identical JEVal accuracy and invalid counts (56.6%, 49/200) with different ECE (0.103 vs 0.075), and near-identical batch curves. That is plausible for a fine-tune of the same base, but it is worth checking that the runs loaded different weights.
